Nobody leases a whole complex, or even a whole phase. Tenants lease floors in a particular building, and the questions that decide the deal are very practical. Is one floor big enough for the team? Where are the labs? Which floors face the street? Where do the plant rooms and services sit?

The drill-down gets visitors to those answers quickly. Three taps take them from a phase to a building and then to its individual floors, and every step they've taken along the way stays visible.

## Why fit is decided floor by floor

Figures for a whole complex or phase tell you how big it is. They don't tell you whether it'll work for you. A phase can have plenty of floor space overall and still not have a single building with floors large enough for a company that wants to sit together on one level.

The drill-down brings things down to the level where people actually make decisions. By the time someone sends an enquiry, they usually know which building suits them and can explain why.

## Going from a phase to a floor

Open a phase and you'll see the parts it's divided into. Each part is a cluster of buildings that belong together.

Open one of those parts and it gets outlined on the map so you can see exactly where it starts and ends. Every building inside it gets a marker showing its use and height, and the left panel lists the same buildings, with a Back link at the top.

![a part of a phase opened: its outline on the map and its buildings in the left panel](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-1a-1.jpg)

Then open a building. You'll see how many floors it has and how big a typical floor is, how the space is split between different uses, and what each floor is used for, from the ground floor up.

![a building opened: its floors and the use of each one](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-building-level.jpg)

Throughout all this, the map shows where you are and the left panel shows how you got there. You can step back one level or jump all the way back out to the whole complex whenever you like.

## The two things tenants look at first

In the building view, two pieces of information do most of the work.

The first is the typical floor plate, which is the area of one standard floor. It tells a tenant how much room they'd have if they took a whole floor, and it's usually the very first thing they check.

The second is the floor-by-floor list, which shows what each level is used for. A lab tenant can see which floors are set up as labs. A retailer can see which ones open onto the street. And anyone can see where the services are, which affects everything from noise to where the lifts go.

Totals can't tell you any of this. Two buildings with the same overall floor area can suit one tenant perfectly and be a poor fit for another, simply because of how their floors are divided.

## Getting to a real conversation sooner

A visitor can work out whether a building fits while they're still standing at the screen, instead of waiting for a follow-up meeting. They walk away with a clear idea of which building works for them.

When they enquire, the commercial team can see which building they opened. That means the first conversation can go straight to terms and timing for that building instead of starting with the basics.
