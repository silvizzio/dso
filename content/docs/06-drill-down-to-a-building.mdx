A tenant considering District IO needs more than the total area of the complex.
They may need to know whether a team can fit on one floor, where the labs are or which spaces face the street.
The location of plant rooms and other services can matter too.

The building view brings those details together.
Visitors move from a phase into its parts and buildings, then examine individual floors while keeping their place on the map.

## Why floor-level detail matters

A phase may contain plenty of space overall without offering the floor size a particular tenant needs.
A company that wants everyone on one level, for example, needs to check the floor plate of a building rather than the phase total.

Looking at individual floors helps visitors identify a suitable building and ask more specific questions when they enquire.

## Opening a building

Start by opening a phase to see its parts, each of which groups buildings together.
Select a part to show its outline on the map.
Markers identify the use and height of each building, and the left panel lists the same buildings with a Back link above them.

![Part of a phase selected, with its boundary on the map and its buildings listed on the left](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-1a-1.jpg)

Open a building to see its floor count, typical floor area and mix of uses.
The floor-by-floor view shows the proposed use of each level, starting at the ground floor.

![A selected building, showing its floors and their proposed uses](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-building-level.jpg)

The map keeps the location visible, while the left panel provides a route back through the selection.
You can return one level or move back to the whole complex.

## Comparing floor sizes and uses

The typical floor plate is the area of a standard floor.
It helps tenants judge how much room a whole floor would provide.

The floor list adds the proposed use of each level.
A lab tenant can identify lab floors, while a retailer can look for space at street level.
The position of services also helps explain the building's layout and raises practical questions about access and neighbouring uses.

Two buildings with similar total floor areas may suit different tenants because their floors are arranged differently.
Reading both the size and the use of each floor gives visitors a more useful basis for comparison.

## Preparing for a discussion

Visitors can use this detail to decide which buildings deserve a closer look before arranging a meeting.
The enquiry includes the selected building, so the commercial team can begin with the visitor's requirements, timing and preferred space.

Sub-phase and building figures are currently indicative.
The [Reference](/dso/docs/17-reference) explains which details still need confirmation from DSO.
