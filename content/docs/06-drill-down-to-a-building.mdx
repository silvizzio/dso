A tenant does not lease a complex or a phase.
They lease floors in a building.
The questions that decide a lease are specific.
Is one floor large enough?
Where are the labs, the shops and the services?

The drill-down answers those questions on the spot.
It takes a visitor from a phase to a building to its floors in three taps, while keeping every earlier step visible.

## Fit is decided at floor level, not complex level

Figures for a whole complex or a whole phase describe scale.
They do not describe fit.
A phase can have ample floor area and still contain no building with floors large enough for a given tenant.

The drill-down moves the conversation to the level where decisions are made.
By the time a visitor enquires, they know which building suits them and why.

## The drill-down keeps context at every level

1. Open a phase to see its parts.
   Each part groups buildings that belong together.
2. Open a part to see its buildings.
   - The part is outlined on the map, so its extent is clear.
   - Each building has a marker with its use and height.
   - The left panel lists the same buildings, with a Back link at the top.

![a part of a phase opened: its outline on the map and its buildings in the left panel](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-1a-1.jpg)

3. Open a building to see how it is organised.
   - How many floors it has, and the size of a typical floor.
   - How its space divides between uses.
   - What each floor is used for, from the ground floor up.

![a building opened: its floors and the use of each one](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-building-level.jpg)

At every step, the map shows where the visitor is and the left panel shows how they got there.
A visitor can go back one level or return to the whole complex at any time.

## Floor-level data answers a tenant's first questions

Two items in the building view carry most of the decision.

- The **typical floor plate** is the area of one standard floor.
  It tells a tenant how much space they get if they take a whole floor, which is usually the first test of fit.
- The floor-by-floor list shows the use of each floor.
  A lab tenant sees where the lab floors are.
  A retailer sees which floors face the street.
  Everyone sees where plant and services sit.

Totals cannot answer these questions.
A building of a given size can suit one tenant and fail another, depending on how its floors are divided.

## The drill-down shortens the path to a specific conversation

For a buyer, fit is judged at the stand, not after a later meeting.
They leave with a clear view of which building works for them.

For the commercial team, a visitor who has opened a building arrives at the enquiry with a specific interest.
The first conversation can go straight to terms and timing for that building.

## Three design decisions shape the drill-down

- Context is never lost.
  Each step keeps the previous one visible, on the map and in the left panel.
- The typical floor plate comes first, because it answers the most common first question.
- Floors are shown one by one, not only as a total, because fit depends on how space is divided.
