Tenants do not lease a whole complex, or even a whole phase. They lease floors in a building, and the questions that decide a lease are very specific. Is a single floor big enough? Where are the labs, the shops and the building services?

The drill-down answers these questions on the spot. In three taps, it takes a visitor from a phase to a building and then to its floors, while keeping every previous step in view.

## Fit is decided floor by floor

Figures for a whole complex or phase tell you about scale, but not about fit. A phase can have plenty of floor space overall and still not contain a single building with floors large enough for a particular tenant.

The drill-down brings the conversation down to the level where real decisions are made. By the time a visitor makes an enquiry, they know which building suits them, and why.

## You never lose your place

1. Open a phase to see its parts. Each part groups together buildings that belong to the same cluster.
2. Open a part to see its buildings.
   - The part is outlined on the map, so its boundaries are clear.
   - Each building has a marker showing its use and height.
   - The left panel lists the same buildings, with a Back link at the top.

![a part of a phase opened: its outline on the map and its buildings in the left panel](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-1a-1.jpg)

3. Open a building to see how it is laid out.
   - How many floors it has, and the size of a typical floor.
   - How its space is split between different uses.
   - What each floor is used for, from the ground floor up.

![a building opened: its floors and the use of each one](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-building-level.jpg)

At every step, the map shows where the visitor is, and the left panel shows how they got there. They can go back one level, or return to the complex as a whole, at any time.

## Floor-level data answers a tenant's first questions

Two pieces of information in the building view do most of the work.

- The **typical floor plate** is the area of one standard floor. It tells a tenant how much space they would have if they took an entire floor, which is usually the first thing they check.
- The **floor-by-floor list** shows what each floor is used for. A lab tenant can see where the lab floors are, a retailer can see which floors face the street, and everyone can see where the plant rooms and services are located.

Totals alone cannot answer these questions. Two buildings of the same size can suit one tenant perfectly and be completely wrong for another, depending on how their floors are divided.

## A faster route to a meaningful conversation

For a buyer, it means they can judge whether a building fits while they are still at the stand, instead of waiting for a follow-up meeting. They leave with a clear idea of which building works for them.

For the commercial team, it means a visitor who has looked inside a building arrives at the enquiry with a specific interest. The first conversation can go straight to terms and timing for that building.

## Design decisions behind the drill-down

- **Context is never lost.** Each step keeps the previous one visible, both on the map and in the left panel.
- **The typical floor plate comes first,** because it answers the question tenants usually ask first.
- **Floors are shown individually, not just as a total,** because fit depends on how the space is divided.
