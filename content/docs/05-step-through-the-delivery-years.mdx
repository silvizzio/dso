District IO will not open all at once. Each phase will be completed in its own year, and the complex will keep growing around its first tenants for several years after they move in. A buyer who only ever sees the finished render has no way of knowing what will actually be there on the day they arrive.

The platform solves this by letting visitors step through the delivery years. They choose a year, and the map shows the phases and buildings that will have been delivered by then, with everything still to come shown as planned. The finished complex becomes a sequence that visitors can follow one year at a time.

![District IO in one delivery year: phases delivered by then in full, later phases shown as planned](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-2030-some-phases-not-built-yet.jpg)

## Why a finished render is not enough

Most off-plan marketing shows only the end result, with every building complete and every street landscaped. That picture is accurate, but only for the final year. For a buyer, the year they move in matters far more than the year the last building is finished.

Stepping through the years answers the question a render cannot: what will be around me when I arrive, and what will still be under construction?

## Each year shows what will exist by then

1. Open District IO in Future. The delivery track appears at the top of the left panel, above the list of phases.
2. Choose a year using the arrows or the year steps.
3. Look at the map for that year.
   - Phases delivered by that year appear as built, together with their buildings.
   - Phases that are still to come appear as planned.
4. Select a delivered phase to see which of its buildings will exist in that year.
5. Move to the next year and watch the next phase appear.

![the delivery track at the top of the left panel, above the phases and their expected completion](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io.jpg)

The list of phases gives the same information in text form. Each phase shows the year it is expected to be completed, so visitors can follow the sequence without moving the timeline at all.

## The delivery track shows where the project stands today

The delivery track sits above the timeline and marks four milestones: concept, start of construction, completion and handover. The current stage is highlighted, so visitors can always see how far the project has progressed.

Two of these milestones are easily confused, and the difference matters to a buyer.

- **Completion** is the date construction is finished.
- **Handover** is the date the tenant or buyer receives the keys. It usually comes a few months after completion, once inspections are done and access for fit-out has been arranged.

The platform shows both, because handover is the date a buyer actually plans around.

## Understanding the sequence helps buyers decide when to arrive

For a buyer, choosing a phase also means choosing a move-in year. An early phase means moving in sooner and having more options, but living alongside construction for longer. A later phase means moving in later, into surroundings that are more complete. Stepping through the years makes this trade-off clear before the visitor commits to anything.

For the commercial team, a buyer who has seen the full sequence has realistic expectations from the start. They are much less likely to feel misled when construction continues around them, and that protects the relationship long after the contract is signed.

## Design decisions that keep the timeline honest

- Built and planned phases look different on the map, so no one mistakes a future phase for one that already exists.
- Delivery years are labelled as indicative until DSO confirms the programme. They are shown as a guide, not a promise.
- The map stays the same from year to year, and only the buildings change, so visitors are always comparing like with like.
