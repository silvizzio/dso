District IO is planned to open in phases, so tenants in an early phase may move in while construction continues elsewhere. The delivery timeline helps visitors look beyond the final render and consider what their surroundings might be like in the year they hope to arrive.

Choosing a year shows the proposed development at that point in the programme. Phase markers retain the **Planned** label and a target year, even where the model shows finished buildings. All dates remain indicative until DSO confirms the programme.

![The 2030 scenario, with phase markers labelled Planned and their target years](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-2030-some-phases-not-built-yet.jpg)

## Choosing a year

Use the Timeline arrows or year steps in the left panel of the Future overview. If you have already opened a phase or building, select Back to return to those controls.

The milestone track above the District IO phase list is a separate part of the interface. It describes the programme's stages; it is not the control for changing the year shown on the map.

![District IO: programme milestones above the phase list and target years](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io.jpg)

Compare the model with the target years on the phase markers, then open a phase to read its **Target Year** and proposed building details. Return to the overview and choose another year to see what is expected to change. The phase list also shows target years if you want to review the sequence without stepping through every map view.

## Understanding completion and handover

The programme track shows Concept, Start, Complete and Handover, with the current stage highlighted. Complete marks the end of construction. Handover is when the buyer or tenant receives the keys, after the inspections and access arrangements needed for occupation or fit-out. The two dates may be months apart.

A visitor planning a move therefore needs to discuss the handover arrangements as well as the construction programme. The Complete milestone on this track does not mean that a proposed phase has already been completed.

## Comparing an earlier and a later move

An earlier phase may suit a tenant who needs space sooner, but nearby building work could continue after they arrive. Waiting for a later phase may mean more of the surrounding development is finished. Comparing the same area across the available years helps visitors identify those differences and prepare questions for the commercial team.

Use the timeline to explain the proposed sequence, rather than as a confirmed promise of readiness. DSO still needs to confirm the dates and the arrangements that would apply to a particular buyer or tenant.
