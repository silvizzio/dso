District IO will not open all at once.
Each phase completes in its own year, and the complex grows around the first tenants for years after they move in.
A buyer who sees only the finished render cannot tell what will stand on the day they arrive.

The platform lets a visitor step through the delivery years.
They choose a year, and the map shows the phases and buildings delivered by then.
Everything still to come appears as planned.
The finished complex becomes a sequence the visitor can follow, year by year.

![District IO in one delivery year: phases delivered by then in full, later phases shown as planned](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-2030-some-phases-not-built-yet.jpg)

## A finished render hides the years that matter most

Most off-plan material shows the end state: every building complete and every street planted.
That picture is accurate for the final year only.
For a buyer, the year they move in matters more than the year the last building completes.

Stepping through the years answers the question a render cannot.
What will stand around me when I arrive, and what will still be under construction?

## Each year on the timeline shows what exists by then

1. Open District IO in Future.
   The left panel shows the delivery track at the top, above the list of phases.
2. Choose a year with the arrows or the year steps.
3. Read the map for that year.
   - Phases delivered by that year appear as built, with their buildings.
   - Phases still to come appear as planned.
4. Select a delivered phase to see which of its buildings exist in that year.
5. Step to the next year and watch the next phase appear.

![the delivery track at the top of the left panel, above the phases and their expected completion](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io.jpg)

The list of phases carries the same information in text.
Each phase shows the year it is expected to complete, so a visitor can read the sequence without moving the timeline.

## The delivery track shows where the project stands today

The delivery track sits above the timeline.
It marks four milestones: concept, construction start, completion and handover.
The current stage is highlighted, so a visitor always knows how far the project has come.

Two of the milestones are easy to confuse, and the difference matters to a buyer.

- **Completion** is the date construction finishes.
- **Handover** is the date a tenant or buyer receives the keys.
  It usually follows completion by some months, once inspections and fit-out access are done.

The platform shows both, because handover is the date a buyer plans around.

## Seeing the sequence helps a buyer choose when to arrive

For a buyer, the choice of phase is also a choice of arrival year.
An early phase means an earlier move and more choice, with construction nearby for longer.
A later phase means a later move into a more complete setting.
Stepping through the years makes this trade-off visible before the visitor commits.

For the commercial team, a buyer who has seen the sequence has realistic expectations.
They are less likely to feel misled when construction continues around them, which protects the relationship after signing.

## Three design decisions keep the timeline honest

- Built and planned look different on the map, so no visitor mistakes a future phase for an existing one.
- Delivery years are marked as indicative until DSO confirms the programme.
  The platform shows them as a guide, not a promise.
- The map stays the same in every year and only the buildings change, so the visitor compares like with like.
