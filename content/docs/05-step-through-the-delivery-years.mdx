District IO is planned for delivery in phases, so its first tenants may move in while work continues elsewhere in the complex.
A render of the completed development does not explain what their surroundings will look like at that point.

The delivery timeline lets visitors explore that difference.
Choosing a year shows the proposed development at that point in the programme.
Phase markers use Planned and a target year, including when the model shows the intended finished buildings.
The dates are indicative until DSO confirms the programme.

![The 2030 scenario, with phase markers labelled Planned and their target years](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-2030-some-phases-not-built-yet.jpg)

## Seeing the development at your move-in date

The finished view shows the intended end result.
For someone considering an earlier move, the more useful question is what will be ready nearby and where construction may still be taking place.
Comparing delivery years helps visitors consider the property in that setting.

## Moving through the years

In the Future overview, use the Timeline arrows or year steps in the left panel to choose a year.
If you have opened the phase or building list, use Back to return to the overview controls.
The milestone track inside District IO describes its programme; it is separate from the year selector.

Compare the buildings shown in the model with the target years on the phase markers.
A rendered building represents the proposal for that scenario, not a record of completed construction.
Select a phase to inspect its proposed buildings and read Target Year in the details panel.
Then move to another year to compare the view.

![District IO: programme milestones above the phase list and target years](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io.jpg)

The phase list also shows target years.
Visitors can read the proposed sequence there without moving through every year on the map.

## Completion and handover are different milestones

The delivery track marks concept, the start of construction, completion and handover, with the current stage highlighted.

Completion refers to the end of construction.
Handover is when a buyer or tenant receives the keys, after the inspections and access arrangements needed for occupation or fit-out.
These dates may be months apart.
Showing both helps visitors understand which milestone relates to their planned move.

## Considering an earlier or later move

An earlier phase may suit someone who needs space sooner and wants more choice, but nearby construction may continue after they arrive.
A later phase means a longer wait, with more of the surrounding development expected to be complete.
The timeline helps visitors weigh these considerations before discussing terms.

For the commercial team, it provides a clear way to explain the proposed sequence and set expectations about ongoing work.
Visitors can ask about a particular year or phase instead of relying on the final render alone.

The Planned labels keep the proposed status visible while visitors explore the model.
Compare the same part of the district across years to see what is expected to change.
Treat Target Year as indicative timing until DSO confirms the programme.
The separate Complete and Handover milestones describe different steps in that programme; neither is a current completion status for a proposed phase.
