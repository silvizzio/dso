District IO won't open all at once. Each phase has its own completion year, and the complex will keep growing around its first tenants for several years after they move in. If all a buyer ever sees is the finished render, they have no idea what the place will actually look like on the day they arrive.

The platform lets visitors step through the delivery years to find out. Pick a year and the map shows the phases and buildings that will be finished by then, with everything still to come shown as planned. Instead of one finished picture, the visitor gets to follow the complex as it grows.

![District IO in one delivery year: phases delivered by then in full, later phases shown as planned](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-2030-some-phases-not-built-yet.jpg)

## The problem with the finished render

Most off-plan marketing shows the end result, with every building done and every street planted. That picture is accurate, but only for the very last year. What a buyer really cares about is the year they move in, and that could look quite different.

Stepping through the years answers the question the render skips over. When I arrive, what will be finished around me, and what will still be a building site?

## How to step through the years

Open District IO in Future. The delivery track sits at the top of the left panel, above the list of phases.

Choose a year using the arrows or the year steps, then look at the map. Phases that will be finished by that year appear as built, along with their buildings. Phases that are still to come appear as planned. If you tap on a finished phase, you can see which of its buildings will be standing in that year.

Then move on to the next year and watch the next phase fill in.

![the delivery track at the top of the left panel, above the phases and their expected completion](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io.jpg)

If you'd rather not move the timeline, the list of phases gives you the same information in text. Each phase shows the year it's expected to be finished, so the sequence is easy to follow at a glance.

## Reading the delivery track

The delivery track sits above the timeline and marks four milestones: concept, start of construction, completion and handover. Whichever stage the project is at gets highlighted, so visitors can always see how far along things are.

Two of these milestones are easy to mix up, and for a buyer the difference matters. Completion is when construction finishes. Handover is when the tenant or buyer actually gets the keys, which is usually a few months later, once inspections are done and access for fit-out has been sorted. The platform shows both because handover is the date people plan their move around.

## Choosing when to arrive

Choosing a phase is also choosing a move-in year, and there's a trade-off either way. Going early means moving in sooner and having more choice of space, but it also means living with construction nearby for longer. Going later means waiting, but arriving somewhere more settled and complete. Stepping through the years lays this out clearly before anyone commits.

It helps the commercial team as well. A buyer who has seen the full sequence knows what to expect and is much less likely to feel misled when building work carries on next door after they move in. That goodwill is worth a lot once the contract is signed.

A few choices keep the timeline honest. Built and planned phases look different on the map, so no one mistakes a future phase for one that already exists. The delivery years are labelled as indicative until DSO confirms the programme, since they're a guide and not a promise. And the map itself stays exactly the same from year to year, with only the buildings changing, so every year can be compared fairly with the last.
