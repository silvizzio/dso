A map shows where a building is, but not what it feels like to arrive there or to stand inside. Getting that impression usually means visiting the site in person, which is not possible for someone standing at an exhibition stand.

The platform brings the site visit to the screen instead. Street view lets visitors walk up to a building at eye level, and the interior tour then takes them inside an available space.

## A site visit, without leaving the stand

A site visit answers questions that no floor plan or photo can. What does the approach look like? How far is it to walk from the road? What is next door? How much daylight does the floor get?

Street view and the interior tour answer these questions right at the stand, giving visitors the kind of impression they would normally only get after a trip to the site.

## Street view starts by dropping a pin

1. Drag the pin from the map controls. The streets where street view is available will light up.

![dragging the pin: the streets with street view light up on the map](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-drag-and-place-pin.jpg)

2. Drop the pin on one of the highlighted streets. The view switches to eye level at that spot.
3. Start walking. Drag to look around, and tap the arrow to move towards the next building.
4. Use the mini map in the corner, which shows where you are standing and which way you are facing.

<DocVideo src="/dso/videos/docs/04-now-lod3-street-view.mp4" caption="street view at eye level, moving along the street towards a building" />

## The interior tour shows the unit itself

1. From an available property, open View interior.
2. Drag to look around the unit.
3. Tap the arrow to move to the next point.

<DocVideo src="/dso/videos/docs/04-now-lod3-virtual-tour-interior.mp4" caption="the interior tour at eye level, walking through a shopping mall past its shops" />

Once inside, visitors can check the things a floor plan leaves out, such as the windows and the view, the ceiling height and how much natural light the space gets.

## A virtual visit moves the sale forward

For visitors, the sense of place that would normally require a trip is available right there and then. They can decide whether a space is worth considering before arranging a viewing.

For the commercial team, a visitor who has already walked the street and toured the interior arrives at the first meeting further along in their decision. The first on-site viewing then confirms a choice instead of starting one.

## Design decisions behind the tours

- **Highlighted streets show where street view is available,** so visitors never drop the pin somewhere with nothing to see.
- **The mini map keeps visitors connected to the main map,** which is easy to lose track of at eye level.
- **Enquire stays on screen throughout every tour,** because a tour is often the moment a visitor makes up their mind.
