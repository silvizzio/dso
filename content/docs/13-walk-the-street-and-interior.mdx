A map tells you where a building is. It doesn't tell you what it's like to walk up to it, or what it feels like to stand inside. Normally you'd need a site visit for that, and someone standing at an exhibition stand can't just pop over.

So the platform brings the site visit to them. Street view lets visitors walk up to a building at eye level, and the interior tour takes them inside a space that's available to lease.

## The questions only a visit can answer

Some things you only find out by being there. What does the approach look like? How far is the walk from the road? What's next door? How much daylight does the floor actually get?

Street view and the interior tour let visitors answer those questions without leaving the stand, and come away with the kind of impression they'd normally only get from a trip to the site.

## Using street view

Drag the pin from the map controls. As you drag it, the streets where street view is available light up.

![dragging the pin: the streets with street view light up on the map](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-drag-and-place-pin.jpg)

Drop the pin on one of those lit-up streets and the view switches to eye level at that spot. From there you can drag to look around and tap the arrow to walk towards the next building. The mini map in the corner shows where you're standing and which way you're facing, which helps a lot because it's easy to lose your bearings at street level.

<DocVideo src="/dso/videos/docs/04-now-lod3-street-view.mp4" caption="street view at eye level, moving along the street towards a building" />

Lighting up only the streets with coverage means nobody drops the pin somewhere and finds there's nothing to see.

## Taking the interior tour

From any available property, open View interior. Drag to look around the space, and tap the arrow to move to the next point.

<DocVideo src="/dso/videos/docs/04-now-lod3-virtual-tour-interior.mp4" caption="the interior tour at eye level, walking through a shopping mall past its shops" />

Inside, visitors can check the things a floor plan doesn't show, like the windows and what you can see from them, how high the ceilings are and how much natural light comes in.

## How it moves the sale along

A visitor gets a real sense of the place on the spot, without having to travel. They can decide whether a space is worth a proper look before booking a viewing.

That means when they do meet the commercial team, they're further along. The first on-site viewing becomes a chance to confirm a choice they've already half made, rather than the start of the search. And since a tour is often the moment someone makes up their mind, the Enquire button stays on screen the whole time.
