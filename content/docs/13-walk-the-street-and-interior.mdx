A map shows where a building is.
It does not show what it feels like to arrive there, or to stand inside.
That impression usually needs a site visit, which a visitor at an exhibition stand cannot make.

The platform brings the site visit to the screen.
Street view lets a visitor walk the approach to a building at eye level.
The interior tour then takes them inside an available space.

## The site visit moves to the screen

A site visit answers questions no plan or photograph can.
What does the approach look like?
How far is the walk from the road?
What sits next door?
How much daylight does the floor get?

Street view and the interior tour answer these questions at the stand.
A visitor forms the impression that usually comes only after a trip to the site.

## Street view starts with a drop of the pin

1. Drag the pin from the map controls.
   The streets where street view is available light up.

![dragging the pin: the streets with street view light up on the map](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-drag-and-place-pin.jpg)

2. Drop the pin on a lit street.
   The view drops to eye level at that point.
3. Walk.
   Drag to look around, and tap the arrow to move towards the next building.
4. Check the mini map in the corner, which shows where the visitor stands and which way they face.

<DocVideo src="/dso/videos/docs/04-now-lod3-street-view.mp4" caption="street view at eye level, moving along the street towards a building" />

## The interior tour shows the unit itself

1. From an available property, open View interior.
2. Drag to look around the unit.
3. Tap the arrow to move to the next point.

{/* Interior tour image to follow: ![the interior tour inside an available space](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-virtual-tour-interior.jpg) */}

Inside, a visitor can check what a floor plan leaves out: the windows and the view, the ceiling height and the amount of daylight.

## A remote site visit moves the sale forward

For a visitor, the sense of place that normally needs a trip is available on the spot.
They can rule a space in or out before arranging a viewing.

For the commercial team, a visitor who has walked the street and toured the interior arrives at the first meeting further along.
The first viewing on site confirms a choice rather than starting one.

## Three design decisions shape the tours

- Lit streets show where street view exists, so a visitor never drops the pin somewhere with nothing to see.
- The mini map keeps the link to the map at eye level, where it is easy to lose orientation.
- Enquire stays on screen during every tour, because a tour is often the moment a visitor decides.
