District IO is DSO's main new offer, and not a single part of it has been built. Anyone looking at it today is looking at a plan, and a plan leaves most of the picturing to the buyer.

Future does a lot of that picturing for them. It drops District IO onto the live map in its actual location, next to buildings that are already standing, so it stops being an abstract drawing. Visitors can move through it one phase at a time, watch it grow year by year, and open up a single building to see what's on each floor. The platform opens on Future by default, since this is where the offer is.

## From a plan on paper to a place you can move through

A printed plan shows a complex from one angle, at one moment in time, with nothing around it. Future changes that in a few ways.

Because the complex sits on the real map, visitors can see who its neighbours will be, which roads lead to it and how far it is from everything else. They also get to set their own pace. Someone can start with the whole complex, narrow it down to a phase, pick a building and then look at individual floors, stopping wherever they like. And they can see how things change over time, with the complex building up phase by phase so it's clear what will be there in any given year.

Taken together, it feels much closer to walking around a finished district than to reading a site plan.

## What Future does for the sale

For a buyer or tenant, Future makes District IO feel real enough to judge. They can find the part of the complex that suits them, see what will be around it, and form an opinion on their own, without an agent leaning over their shoulder.

By the time someone taps Enquire in Future, they've usually looked around, compared a few options and settled on something specific. That's good news for the commercial team, because the first conversation can start from that choice rather than from "so, what are you looking for?"

## How a visit through Future usually goes

1. [Explore District IO](/dso/docs/04-explore-district-io): break the complex into its phases and find the one that fits.
2. [Step Through the Delivery Years](/dso/docs/05-step-through-the-delivery-years): move through the years to see which phases and buildings will be finished when.
3. [Drill Down to a Building](/dso/docs/06-drill-down-to-a-building): go from a phase to a building to a floor and check whether it works.
4. [Enquire Off-Plan](/dso/docs/07-enquire-off-plan): register interest from exactly where the visitor has stopped.

The steps follow the way questions come up naturally. Once someone has found a phase they like, they want to know what will be around it. Once they know that, they want to see inside the building. And once they've seen inside, they're ready to get in touch.

## Keeping Future believable

Every figure in Future is marked as projected. The platform never presents a planned number as if it were a fact, because if a buyer caught one figure being oversold, they'd start doubting all the others.

Detail also comes in small steps. Each screen shows only what the visitor needs for their next choice, which keeps a complex this size from feeling overwhelming.

And although Future opens first, Now and Past are always one tap away. A visitor can check the offer against what exists today, or against what DSO has already delivered, whenever they want to.
