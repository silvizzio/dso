Each phase of District IO completes at a different time.
A buyer who moves into an early phase will live or work beside construction while later phases are built.
A buyer choosing a later phase cannot easily picture which neighbours will already be there.

Plans and brochures show the finished complex, which hides this.
The platform adds time as a dimension.
A visitor moves the timeline, and the complex on the map changes to show what exists in that year.

![the complex at one point in time: finished phases in full, later phases shown as planned](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-2030-some-phases-not-built-yet.jpg)

## Time is the missing dimension in off-plan sales

Most off-plan material shows the end state: every building complete, every street planted.
That picture is accurate for the final year and misleading for every year before it.
For a buyer, the year they move in matters more than the year the last building completes.

Showing the complex year by year answers the question a finished render cannot: what will it be like when I arrive?

## The timeline shows the complex at any point in its delivery

1. Read the delivery track at the top of the left panel.
   It shows where the project stands today.
2. Move the timeline, using the arrows or the year steps.
3. Watch the map change.
   - Phases finished by that year appear in full.
   - Phases not yet finished appear as planned.
4. Select a phase at that point in time to see its details for that stage.

![the delivery track at the top of the left panel, above the list of phases](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io.jpg)

## The delivery track separates completion from handover

The delivery track shows four milestones: concept, construction start, completion and handover.
Two of them are easy to confuse, and the difference matters to a buyer.

- **Completion** is the date construction finishes.
- **Handover** is the date a tenant or buyer receives the keys.
  It usually follows completion by some months, once inspections and fit-out access are done.

The platform shows both, because handover is the date a buyer plans around.
A track that showed only completion would suggest an earlier arrival than the buyer will get.

## Seeing the sequence builds trust on both sides

For a buyer, the question of what will surround them gets a visual answer.
They choose knowing which neighbours will exist on arrival and which will still be under construction.

For DSO, showing the sequence openly prevents surprises later.
A buyer who understood the phasing when they enquired is less likely to feel misled when construction continues around them.

## Three design decisions keep the timeline honest

- Planned and built phases look different on the map, so no visitor confuses a future phase with an existing one.
- Delivery dates are marked as indicative until DSO confirms the programme.
  The platform shows them as a guide, not a promise.
- The same map is used for every year.
  Only the buildings change, so the visitor compares like with like.
