Visitors may encounter the Digital Twin at an exhibition stand or in a sales centre, with little time or guidance.
The interface needs to help them get started while giving interested visitors room to explore.

Rather than showing every building, figure and video at once, the platform introduces detail as people zoom in or select a property.
The layout stays consistent, so they can explore further without having to learn a different set of controls.

## How the layout works

The map fills the screen, with panels arranged around its edges to keep the selected location in view.
The left panel holds the choices, while the right panel explains the selection and provides the available actions.
Visitors can select a property from the list or tap it on the map.

The opening view contains only the information needed to understand the district at a glance.
More detail appears in response to a selection, giving visitors control over how much they see.

## The three zoom levels

Each level serves a different purpose.
Visitors move between them by zooming or selecting a property.

### Level 1: the whole district

![Level 1: the whole district with a strip of headline figures](/dso/api/img/02-lod1.jpg)

The opening view shows the whole district and a strip of headline figures along the bottom.
Small icons accompany the figures, and there are no side panels to navigate.
This view is intended to be readable from a short distance and invite people to approach the screen.

### Level 2: getting your bearings

![Level 2: the district core, with District IO at the centre](/dso/api/img/02-lod2-dso-1.jpg)

The second level brings the district core into view, with District IO among the existing buildings.
Visitors can understand where the proposed complex sits and what surrounds it before opening detailed property information.

### Level 3: exploring a property

![Level 3: a selected property, with its spaces on the left and details on the right](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-available-technohub-4.jpg)

The district overview leads into detailed property views at LOD 3.
Visitors can explore properties, compare details and enquire.
Future and Now include district overviews as well as closer property views; Past brings the archive timeline and completion records together at LOD 3.

## What each part of the screen does

| Area | Purpose | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|
| Left panel | Choose what the map shows | Keeps selection controls together |
| Map | Show the district and the selected property | Keeps the location visible while visitors explore |
| Right panel | Explain the selection and offer relevant actions | Puts property information beside the next steps |
| Map controls | Change the zoom, basemap or street view | Keeps view controls separate from property details |
| Header | Show the date, time and district conditions | Provides context for the current view |

When a visitor opens a property, the left panel lists its contents and provides a Back link.
They can explore further and still see how to return to the previous level.

The map and list stay linked.
Selecting a property on the map highlights it in the list, while selecting it in the list moves the map to that location.
Both routes open the same information.

## Using the left panel

The district overview groups the controls under Exploration Mode, Timeline, Areas of Interest and Assets.
Opening a property replaces the overview choices with its history, phases or units, with a Back link above them.
Free, Business and Research remain the exploration choices.
The timeline changes the period shown, while Areas of Interest gives visitors a direct route to a featured property.
Asset categories such as Office, Residential and Mobility help them narrow what they see on the map.

## Reading the right panel

Property overview panels use a similar order; building-data and archive views have their own layouts.
The status and name come first, followed by images, a short description and the main figures.
Video or camera tiles follow where provided.
Actions sit at the bottom. Depending on the selection, these include Enquire, Building Info and View interior.

Keeping this order consistent makes properties easier to compare.
Once visitors know where to find a detail, they can look in the same place on the next property.

## Understanding status labels

Each property or space has a status label that explains its availability or stage of development.

| Label | Meaning | What the visitor can do |
|---|---|---|
| Available | Space offered for lease | Explore the property, take the interior tour and enquire |
| Under offer | An offer is in progress but the lease is not yet signed | Register interest in case the space becomes available |
| Leased | No space is currently available | Review the building and discuss future availability with the team |
| Planned | A proposed property or phase with a target year | Explore the proposal and make an off-plan enquiry |
| Completed | A completed property shown in Past with its completion year | Review it as part of DSO's delivery record |

The label helps visitors understand what they are looking at before reading the details.
It also determines which actions are relevant to that property.

## Finding the Enquire button

Enquire appears at the bottom of the Future property views and the available-space view in Now.
The exterior and office-interior tours also show an Enquire button.
The supplied leased-building and archive screens focus on viewing information, so do not assume the same action is available on every screen.
After reviewing Past, return to a proposed or available property to open its enquiry form.
