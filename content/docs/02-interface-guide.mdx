Most visitors meet the Digital Twin standing at an exhibition stand or a sales centre, with no guidance and little time.
The platform holds a whole district of buildings, data and media.
Shown all at once, that volume makes a visitor walk away within seconds.

The interface solves this by revealing detail in stages and keeping the same layout at every stage.
A visitor learns the screen once, in the first minute, and can then go as deep as they want without learning anything new.

## Three principles govern every screen

1. The map is the product.
   It fills the screen and stays the main object.
   Panels sit at the edges and never cover the place the visitor is looking at.
2. The left side chooses and the right side explains.
   A visitor decides what to see on the left or on the map.
   The right panel then explains what they chose.
3. Detail follows interest.
   The first view is calm.
   More detail appears only when the visitor zooms in or selects something.

Each principle removes a question the visitor would otherwise have to answer.
The first removes "where do I look".
The second removes "where do I read about this".
The third removes "which of all this matters to me".

## Detail grows across three levels of detail

The map has three levels of detail.
Each level has one job in the visit, and the visitor moves between them by zooming or by selecting a property.

### The first level attracts a visitor from a distance

![the first level: the whole district and a single band of headline figures](/dso/api/img/02-lod1.jpg)

The first level shows the whole district and one band of headline figures across the bottom.
It has no panels to learn.
Each figure has a row of small icons, so the band reads from several steps away.
Its job is to make a passer-by stop and touch the screen.

### The second level orients the visitor around District IO

![the second level: the district core, with District IO at the centre](/dso/api/img/02-lod2-dso-1.jpg)

The second level moves closer to the district core, where District IO sits among the existing buildings.
Its job is orientation.
The visitor sees where the new complex sits, and what surrounds it, before any detail competes for attention.

### The third level opens the full interface

![the third level: left panel, map, map controls and right panel](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-overview.jpg)

The third level opens the full interface.
This is where the visitor explores, compares and acts.
Every chapter in the Future, Now and Past sections takes place at this level.

## Each region of the screen has one job

| Region | Job | Why it works this way |
|---|---|---|
| Left panel | Decides what the map shows | Keeps every choice in one place |
| Map | Shows the district and what is selected | Keeps the visitor oriented at all times |
| Right panel | Explains what is selected and holds the actions | Keeps reading and acting in one place |
| Map controls | Change the view: zoom, basemap, timeline, street view | Keep view changes apart from content |
| Header | Shows the date, time and live conditions in the district | Signals that the map shows the present, not a recording |

The left panel also carries the path back.
When a visitor opens a property, the panel becomes a list of what is inside it, with a Back link at the top.
Going deeper never removes the way out, so a visitor never feels trapped in a sub-screen.

The map and the left panel stay in sync.
Selecting a property on the map highlights it in the list, and selecting it in the list moves the map.
Visitors who think in places and visitors who think in lists both reach the same result.

## The right panel always reads in the same order

1. Status and name, so the visitor knows what they selected and whether it is available.
2. Images, so they see it before reading about it.
3. A short description of what it is and what it is for.
4. Key figures, for visitors who want the scale.
5. Media: videos, and live cameras for buildings that exist.
6. Actions: Enquire, and where available, building data and the interior tour.

A fixed order turns the right panel into a habit.
A visitor who has read one panel knows where to look on the next.
The time from selecting a property to understanding it gets shorter.

## Status labels tell a visitor what they can do next

Every property and every space carries one **status label**, a one-word summary of whether it exists and whether it can be taken.

| Label | Meaning | What the visitor can do |
|---|---|---|
| Available | Space that can be leased now | Inspect it, tour the interior and enquire |
| Under offer | An offer is in progress | Enquire in case the offer does not complete |
| Leased | No space is free now | Enquire for future availability |
| Coming | Not built, construction not started | Explore it and enquire off-plan |
| Completed | Shown in Past, with the year of completion | Read it as part of the delivery record |

The label answers the visitor's first question before they ask it: can I have this?
It also sets their next step, so the interface never offers an action that does not apply.

## The Enquire action never moves

Enquire sits at the bottom of every right panel and stays on screen during street view and interior tours.
It is the one action that is available in every timeline state and at every level of detail.

The reason is commercial.
The value of the platform is lost if a visitor has to search for the way to act at the moment they decide.
A fixed position means the action is found without thinking.
