Satellite imagery shows that a district has grown, but it doesn't tell you what was built, what kind of building it was, or when it was finished. A careful buyer wants the impression and the facts behind it.

So alongside the archive, the platform keeps a proper record of what was completed and when. Visitors can read DSO's track record as well as see it.

![the archive view: the record of completions sits in the right panel beside the imagery](/dso/api/img/05-past-lod3-2020.jpg)

## Putting names to the pictures

The record lists every completed property with the year it was finished. It names what the imagery can only show. With both on screen, a visitor can match each building on the map to the year DSO delivered it.

## Reading the record

Open the archive view in Past. In the right panel you'll find a chart of buildings over time, which shows how quickly the district has grown. Below it are the development records, where each completion is listed with its year and the most recent one carries a New label.

Once you've read through it, head back to Future. You'll be looking at the offer with a much better reason to believe in it.

## How the record connects to District IO

The record covers the same kinds of property District IO brings together on a bigger scale: headquarters, campus offices and labs, and industrial space. A buyer can see that DSO has already built and run every one of those.

That changes the question a buyer is asking. It's no longer "can DSO actually deliver buildings like this?" It's just "when will District IO be ready?"

## The last step in the case

Delivery risk can now be judged on facts: what was built, what type of building it is and how it's being used today.

For DSO, the record finishes the argument that Future started. The offer has been shown, checked against the present and now checked against the past.

The chart and the list work as a pair. The chart shows the pace of growth, and the list names each project, so visitors get the trend and the detail together. The most recent delivery is marked because it's the best evidence of what DSO can deliver right now.
