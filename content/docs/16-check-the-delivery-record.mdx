Imagery shows that a district grew.
It does not say what was built, of what kind, or when.
A careful buyer wants both the impression and the facts.

Alongside the archive, the platform keeps a structured record of what was completed and when.
A visitor can read the track record as well as see it.

![the archive view: the record of completions sits in the right panel beside the imagery](/dso/api/img/05-past-lod3-2020.jpg)

## The record turns imagery into evidence

The record lists each completed property with its year.
It names what the imagery only shows.
With both in view, a visitor can link each building on the map to the date DSO delivered it.

## Reading the record takes four steps

1. Open the archive view in Past.
2. Read the chart of buildings over time, which shows the pace of the district's growth.
3. Read the development records.
   Each completion appears with its year, and the most recent carries a New label.
4. Return to Future, with a reason to believe the offer.

## The record connects DSO's past to District IO

The record covers the same kinds of property that District IO brings together at a larger scale.
They are headquarters, campus offices and labs, and industrial space.
A buyer sees that DSO has already built and run each kind.

This is the link the record makes for a buyer.
The question is no longer whether DSO can deliver buildings of this type.
It is only when District IO will be delivered.

## The record serves the final step of the sales conversation

For a buyer, delivery risk is judged against facts: what was built, of what kind and how it is used today.

For DSO, the record closes the case that Future opened.
The offer has been shown, tested against the present and now against the past.

## Two design decisions shape the record

- The chart shows the pace and the list names the deliveries, so a visitor gets both the trend and the facts.
- The most recent delivery is marked, because it is the strongest evidence of what DSO can deliver now.
