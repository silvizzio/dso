Descriptions of growth are easy to write and easy to discount.
A visitor who is told a district grew steadily has no way to check it.

The archive lets them see it.
Moving the timeline back swaps the map for satellite imagery taken in the chosen year.
Stepping forward again, the visitor watches roads, plots and buildings appear.

## Real imagery makes growth hard to dispute

The archive does not reconstruct the past.
It shows photographs of the site taken at the time.
What a visitor sees for a given year is what was there that year.

That is the source of its value.
A render of past growth would prove nothing.
A photograph does.

## Stepping through the archive takes four steps

1. Switch the timeline to Past.
2. Choose a year, using the year steps or the arrows.
3. Read the change.
   - The map shows the site as photographed that year.
   - The left panel lists only what stood then, each with its year of completion.
   - The right panel switches to the record of the district.
4. Step forward, year by year, to watch the district fill in.

## Everything on screen follows the chosen year

When the year changes, the map, the panels and the record change together.
A visitor never sees a building from one year on imagery from another.
Consistency is what makes the archive trustworthy.

Past also removes what did not exist.
It shows no live cameras, no building data and no leasing status, because none of these existed for an earlier year.
District IO does not appear, because no part of it stood before today.
