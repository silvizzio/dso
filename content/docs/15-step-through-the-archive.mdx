Anyone can write that a district has grown steadily over the years, and anyone reading it can shrug it off. There's no way to check a sentence like that.

The archive gives visitors a way to check. Moving the timeline back swaps the live map for satellite imagery taken in the year you've chosen. Step forward again and you can watch roads, plots and buildings appear one after another.

## Why real photos matter here

The archive doesn't try to recreate the past. It shows real photographs of the site taken at the time, so whatever you see for a given year is what was actually there that year.

That's the whole reason it works. A render showing how the district grew wouldn't prove anything, because anyone can draw a render. A photo from the time is a different matter.

## How to use the archive

Switch the timeline to Past, then pick a year using the year steps or the arrows.

When you do, the map changes to show the site as it was photographed that year. The left panel lists only the buildings that existed at the time, each with the year it was finished, and the right panel switches to the district's delivery record.

From there, step forward one year at a time and watch the district fill in.

## Everything stays in the same year

When you change the year, the map, the panels and the record all change with it. You'll never see a building from one year sitting on imagery from another, and that consistency is a big part of why the archive can be trusted.

Past also leaves out anything that didn't exist yet. There are no live cameras, no building data and no leasing status, since none of that existed back then. District IO doesn't appear at all, because no part of it has been built.
