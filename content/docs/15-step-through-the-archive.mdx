It is easy to write that a district has grown, and just as easy for a reader to dismiss it. A visitor who is simply told that a district has grown steadily has no way of checking whether that is true.

The archive lets them see it for themselves. Moving the timeline back replaces the map with satellite imagery captured in the chosen year. As the visitor steps forward again, they can watch roads, plots and buildings appear.

## Real imagery makes the growth hard to argue with

The archive does not recreate the past. It shows actual photographs of the site taken at the time, so what a visitor sees for any given year is exactly what was there in that year.

That is where its value lies. A render of how the district grew would prove nothing, but a photograph does.

## How to step through the archive

1. Switch the timeline to Past.
2. Choose a year using the year steps or the arrows.
3. See what has changed.
   - The map shows the site as it was photographed that year.
   - The left panel lists only what existed at the time, each with its year of completion.
   - The right panel switches to the district's delivery record.
4. Step forward one year at a time and watch the district fill in.

## Everything on screen matches the chosen year

When the year changes, the map, the panels and the record all update together. A visitor will never see a building from one year placed on imagery from another, and that consistency is what makes the archive trustworthy.

Past also leaves out anything that did not exist yet. There are no live cameras, no building data and no leasing status, because none of these existed in earlier years. District IO does not appear either, because no part of it has been built yet.
