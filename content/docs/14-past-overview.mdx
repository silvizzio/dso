Every off-plan buyer takes on some **delivery risk**: the risk that a building will be late, will change from the original plan, or will never be built at all. The most convincing answer to that risk is the developer's track record of what it has already delivered.

Past shows that track record. It takes the district back to earlier years, using satellite imagery captured at the time, and shows what DSO built and when.

## A track record is the best answer to off-plan risk

Any developer can claim to have a strong track record. It is much harder to argue with a district that visibly fills in year after year, in images taken at the time. Past turns DSO's history from a claim into something visitors can check for themselves.

## Seeing the record makes buying off-plan feel less risky

For buyers, being able to see what DSO has delivered in the past makes committing to District IO feel much less of a leap. They are being asked to trust a developer whose growth they have just watched unfold on screen.

For DSO, the district's history becomes part of the case for District IO, and it makes that case without the commercial team having to say a word.

## What visitors can do in Past

1. [Step Through the Archive](/dso/docs/15-step-through-the-archive): go back in time and watch the district take shape.
2. [Check the Delivery Record](/dso/docs/16-check-the-delivery-record): see what was completed, and when.

The archive shows how the district grew, and the record lists exactly what was delivered. Together, they give visitors both the overall impression and the hard facts.

## How Past stays credible

- **The imagery is real, not rendered.** The archive uses photographs of the site taken at the time.
- **Only what existed at the time is shown.** Past shows only the buildings that stood in the selected year, and no live data, because none existed back then.
