Every off-plan buyer carries **delivery risk**: the risk that a building is late, changes from the plan or is never built.
The strongest answer to that risk is the developer's record of what it has already delivered.

Past shows that record.
It rolls the district back to earlier years, using satellite imagery taken in those years, and shows what DSO built and when.

## The delivery record is the strongest answer to off-plan risk

A developer can claim a strong record.
Seeing a district fill in, year by year, from photographs taken at the time, is much harder to dispute.
Past turns DSO's history from a claim into something a visitor can inspect.

## The record reduces the perceived risk of buying off-plan

For a buyer, visible past delivery makes the decision on District IO feel less exposed.
They are asked to trust a developer whose growth they have just watched on screen.

For DSO, the district's history becomes part of the case for District IO.
It is made without a word from the commercial team.

## A visit through Past covers two tasks

1. [Step Through the Archive](/dso/docs/15-step-through-the-archive): move back in time and watch the district appear.
2. [Check the Delivery Record](/dso/docs/16-check-the-delivery-record): read what was completed, and when.

The archive shows the growth.
The record names what was delivered.
Together they give both the impression and the facts.

## Two rules keep Past credible

- Imagery is real, not rendered.
  The archive uses photographs of the site taken at the time.
- Only what existed appears.
  Past shows only the buildings that stood in the chosen year, and no live data, because none existed then.
