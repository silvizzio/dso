Anyone buying off-plan takes on what's called delivery risk. The building might be late, it might end up different from the plans, or in the worst case it might never be built. The most convincing thing a developer can offer against that worry is a record of what it has already finished.

Past shows that record. It rolls the district back to earlier years using satellite imagery captured at the time, so visitors can see what DSO built and when.

## A record you can actually see

Any developer can say it has a strong track record. It's much harder to argue with a district you can watch filling in, year after year, in images taken at the time. Past turns DSO's history from something the sales team says into something visitors can check for themselves.

## Why it makes off-plan feel less risky

For a buyer, seeing what DSO has already built makes committing to District IO feel like much less of a leap. They're being asked to trust a developer whose growth they've just watched unfold on the screen in front of them.

For DSO, the district's own history becomes part of the pitch for District IO, and it makes that point without anyone from the commercial team having to say a word.

## What you can do in Past

1. [Step Through the Archive](/dso/docs/15-step-through-the-archive): go back in time and watch the district take shape.
2. [Check the Delivery Record](/dso/docs/16-check-the-delivery-record): see what was finished, and when.

The archive shows how the district grew. The record lists exactly what was delivered. Between them, visitors get both the overall impression and the facts to back it up.

## Keeping Past honest

The imagery is real. The archive uses actual photographs of the site taken at the time, not renders.

Past also only shows what existed in the year you've picked. You won't see any live data there, because none of it existed back then.
