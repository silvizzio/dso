People are most interested in a property at the exact moment they find one that suits them. Once they step away from the screen, that interest drops off fast. The traditional approach to off-plan sales asks them to follow up later through a website, a phone call or an email, and a lot of them never do.

The platform catches that interest while it's still there. The Enquire button is available on every phase, part and building, and it opens a short form that already knows what the visitor was looking at.

![the enquiry form over the map, carrying the item the visitor selected](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-1a-form.jpg)

## An enquiry is a question, not a commitment

An off-plan enquiry simply registers interest in a property that hasn't been built yet. There's no reservation, no deposit and no contract involved. It opens a conversation with the DSO commercial team about terms, timing and availability, and that's all.

Keeping it this light is intentional. People think twice before committing to anything, but they'll happily ask a question. The easier the first step feels, the more visitors actually take it.

## Sending an enquiry

Tap Enquire on whatever you're looking at. It could be the whole complex, a single phase, one part of a phase or a particular building.

The form opens on top of the map, and the item you selected is already shown at the top along with its key details. Fill in your name, company, email and phone number. If you want to, add a short note about what you need, for example how much floor space, what you'd use it for or when you'd want to move in. Then hit send, and the enquiry goes to the DSO commercial team with the selected item attached.

If a visitor would rather finish later, they can scan the QR code on screen and carry on from their own phone.

## Why the form is so short

Every extra field on a form loses a few more people. So this one asks only for the four details the commercial team needs to get back to someone, plus an optional message.

The selected item fills in everything else. The team never has to ask what the person was interested in, because the enquiry already tells them.

## What the sales team gets

From the visitor's side, getting in touch takes a few seconds. There's no website to hunt down later and nothing to explain twice.

From the team's side, each enquiry turns up with its context already attached. They can see straight away whether someone asked about the whole complex or about one floor in one building, and they can reply to that specific question instead of sending a general brochure.

Two other details help. The Enquire button is always in the same place at the bottom of the right panel, so nobody has to look for it. And the map stays visible behind the form, so visitors never feel they've been taken away from what they were looking at.
