Dubai Silicon Oasis (DSO) has run a free zone for technology companies since 2005, and most of its built property is leased.
Its next development, District IO, is at concept design stage.
Every buyer or tenant who commits to it today commits to a building they cannot visit.

The DSO Digital Twin replaces that leap of faith with evidence.
It places District IO on a live 3D map of the district.
It sets the new complex beside the property DSO runs today and the property it has delivered.
It then captures an enquiry at the moment a visitor finds something that fits.

## Off-plan sales depend on trust that brochures cannot build

A buyer who commits before a building exists is buying **off-plan**: on the strength of drawings, figures and the developer's record.
Off-plan sales usually rest on a brochure, a set of renders and a conversation with a sales agent.
Each of these leaves a gap that a careful buyer notices.

1. Renders show a building without its surroundings.
   A buyer cannot see what sits next to it or how they would arrive.
   They also cannot see the area as it will be on the day they move in.
2. Claims of delivery are hard to check.
   A buyer is asked to trust that the complex will be built, with little visible proof of what the developer has built before.
3. Interest fades between the first visit and the follow-up.
   A visitor who is curious at the stand must act later, through another channel, and many never do.

## The platform closes each gap with one design decision

| Gap | Design decision | What the visitor gets |
|---|---|---|
| No surroundings | Every building sits on one live map of the whole district | The offer in its real place, next to real neighbours |
| No proof | A timeline switches the map between the planned district, today and earlier years | The offer checked against what runs and what was delivered |
| No follow-through | Every property, in every state of the timeline, has the same Enquire action | A way to act at the moment of interest |

These three decisions shape every screen described in this guide.
The rest of the guide explains how each one works in practice.

## The platform creates value for buyers, for the commercial team and for DSO as operator

### Buyers and tenants judge the offer on evidence

A buyer sees District IO in context, compares its phases and inspects a single building down to its floors.
They then check it against buildings that already run, with live data and tours, and against the record of past delivery.
They act when they are ready, without waiting for an agent.

### The commercial team receives informed, specific enquiries

Every visitor meets the same sales narrative, in the same order, whoever is on the stand.
Each enquiry arrives with context: the team knows whether the visitor looked at the whole complex, one phase or one building.
The first conversation starts from what the visitor already knows.

### DSO shows the district as a place that works

Brochures describe quality.
The platform shows it through live building data, live cameras and tours of available space.
The operating buildings become part of the case for District IO.

## The timeline follows the order of a sales conversation

A sales conversation moves from the offer to the proof to the request for action.
The timeline follows the same order.

1. The widest view shows the scale of the district before any detail appears.
2. Future presents the offer: District IO, from the whole complex down to a single floor.
3. Now shows proof in the present: what DSO runs, how well it runs, and what is free to lease.
4. Past shows proof over time: what DSO has delivered, year by year.
5. Enquire, available everywhere, turns interest into a lead.

This is why the timeline opens on Future and runs to Now and then Past.
A visitor comes for what they can have.
The present and the past then answer the question every off-plan buyer asks: can I believe this will be built?

## Five terms carry the rest of this guide

- A **digital twin** is a live 3D model of a real place, connected to data about it.
  Here, it is the model of Dubai Silicon Oasis that the platform displays.
- A **timeline state** is one of the three versions of the district the platform can show: Future, Now or Past.
- A **level of detail (LOD)** is one of three zoom levels.
  Each level shows more of the interface than the one before.
- An **area of interest** is a property the platform features: District IO, DSO HQ, TechnoHub 4 or LIU 8.
- A **phase** is a part of District IO that is designed, built and handed over as one package.
