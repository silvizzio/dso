Dubai Silicon Oasis (DSO) is a free zone for technology companies in Dubai. It has hosted tenants since 2005. Three of its buildings feature in the kiosk: DSO HQ, TechnoHub 4 and LIU 8. Two of the three are fully leased.

The next step is **District IO**, a 595,300 m² mixed-use complex in five phases. It is at concept design stage: the mix of uses, the phases and the floor areas are set, but the buildings do not exist yet. Anyone who leases or buys there now does so off-plan, on the strength of drawings, figures and the developer's record.

That is the gap the DSO Digital Twin closes. How do you judge a place you cannot walk through, and how do you trust that it will be built? The kiosk answers with three kinds of evidence on one screen:

- **What District IO will contain**, down to one building and its floors.
- **What DSO runs today**, with live building data, cameras and tours.
- **What DSO has delivered before**, year by year since 2005.

When you have seen enough, you send an enquiry from the property you are looking at.

## The kiosk tells the story in five beats

A good sales conversation moves from the offer to the proof to the ask. The kiosk follows the same order, so a visitor who only follows the default screens still hears the whole story.

1. **Hook**, at LOD 1. The scale of the district: homes, units, transport and parking.
2. **Offer**, in Future. What District IO contains, phase by phase, down to one building.
3. **Proof today**, in Now. What exists, how it performs, and what you can lease.
4. **Proof of record**, in Past. What DSO has delivered since 2005.
5. **Action**, in every state. Send your details and the property you looked at.

This explains why the timeline runs Future, Now, Past, and not the other way round. A visitor comes for what they can have, so the kiosk shows the offer first. The present and the past then work as proof.

## District IO is the main offer

District IO is Phase 3 of the DSO masterplan, also called the DSO HQ Complex. CPG Middle East designed it. It joins five plots into one site.

| Figure | Value |
|---|---|
| Gross floor area (GFA) | 595,300 m² |
| Gross leasable area (GLA) | 429,000 m² |
| Plot area | 164,420 m², five amalgamated plots |
| Homes | 704 |
| Hotel keys | 440 |
| Maximum height | G+40 |
| Stage | Concept design |

Three things stand out in these figures:

- **It is a workplace first.** Office and techno lab space is 69% of the floor area.
  - Techno lab space is built for light research and prototyping as well as office work.
  - This suits the technology companies DSO serves.
- **It is also a place to live and stay.** The 704 homes and the 440 key hotel keep the complex active after office hours and at weekends.
- **It is dense.** The floor area is about 3.6 times the plot area, and the tallest building reaches G+40 (a ground floor plus 40 floors).

### GFA and GLA are not the same

The two area figures measure different things, and the difference matters when you compare offers.

- **Gross floor area (GFA)** is all the floor inside the buildings.
- **Gross leasable area (GLA)** is the part a tenant can rent.
  - In District IO, 72% of the floor area is leasable.
  - The rest is lobbies, lifts, stairs, corridors and plant rooms.

When you compare two properties, compare GLA with GLA.

## How to use this guide

The guide follows the kiosk. Each timeline state is a section, and each chapter inside it is one thing you can do.

- **If you want to lease or buy**, read the sections in kiosk order:
  1. [Future](/dso/docs/03-future-overview): what District IO offers, and how to enquire.
  2. [Now](/dso/docs/08-now-overview): what you can lease today, and how it performs.
  3. [Past](/dso/docs/14-past-overview): what DSO has delivered.
- **If you run or support the kiosk**, read:
  - The [Interface Guide](/dso/docs/02-interface-guide) for every control on screen.
  - The [Reference](/dso/docs/17-reference) for where each figure comes from, and which figures are still indicative.
