Marketing photos show a building at its very best, often before anyone has moved in. Visitors know this, and they tend to take such images with a pinch of salt.

The platform adds two kinds of media that tackle this in different ways. Live cameras show buildings exactly as they are right now, and videos tell the story of the district in a format that works well on a large touch screen.

## Live cameras show the building as it really is

A live feed cannot be staged. When a visitor opens a camera pointed at an entrance or a lobby, they see it exactly as it is at that moment, including its condition, how busy it is and the people using it.

To open a camera:

1. Find the camera tiles in the right panel of any existing property. Each one is marked as live.
2. Tap a tile to open the camera in full screen, with its name, location and live status.

![a live camera open full screen, with its name and live status](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-cctv-enlarge.jpg)

## Videos tell the district's story

Videos cover what a map cannot show: the everyday activity, the events and the people who work in the district. They play without narration and hold attention well on a large screen, which makes them ideal for visitors standing at an exhibition stand.

To watch the videos:

1. Tap a video tile in the right panel.
2. Switch between videos using the thumbnail strip below the player.

![the video gallery open full screen, with the thumbnails below](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-video-enlarge.jpg)

## Media builds trust and keeps people engaged

For visitors, live cameras close the gap between marketing and reality, while the videos give them a feel for the district as a working community.

For DSO, the two together keep visitors on the platform for longer, and the longer someone stays, the more likely they are to explore further and make an enquiry.

## Design decisions behind the media

- **Live feeds are always labelled,** so visitors know whether they are watching what is happening now or a produced video.
- **Media opens over the map and closes back to the same spot,** so visitors never lose their place.
