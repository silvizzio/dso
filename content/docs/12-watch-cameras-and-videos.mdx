Marketing photographs show a building at its best moment, often before it was occupied.
A visitor knows this, and discounts what they see.

The platform adds two kinds of media that address this in different ways.
Live cameras show buildings as they are right now.
Videos tell the story of the district in a form that works on a large touch screen.

## Live cameras show the building as it is

A live feed cannot be staged.
A visitor who opens a camera on an entrance or a lobby sees it as it is at that moment.
Its condition, its activity and its people are all visible.

To open a camera:

1. Find the camera tiles in the right panel of any built property.
   Each is marked as live.
2. Tap a tile to open the camera full screen, with its name, location and live status.

![a live camera open full screen, with its name and live status](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-cctv-enlarge.jpg)

## Videos carry the district's story

Videos cover what a map cannot: activity, events and the people who work in the district.
They run without narration and hold attention on a large screen, which suits a visitor standing at a stand.

To watch the videos:

1. Tap a video tile in the right panel.
2. Move between videos with the thumbnail strip below the player.

![the video gallery open full screen, with the thumbnails below](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-video-enlarge.jpg)

## Media answers doubt and holds attention

For a visitor, live cameras remove the gap between marketing and reality.
Videos give a sense of the district as a working community.

For DSO, the two together keep visitors at the platform longer.
The longer a visitor stays, the more likely they are to explore further and enquire.

## Two design decisions shape the media

- Live feeds are always labelled, so a visitor knows whether they are watching the present or a produced video.
- Media opens over the map and closes back to the same place, so a visitor never loses where they were.
