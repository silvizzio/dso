Marketing photos show a building on its best day, often before anyone has even moved in. Visitors know this, so they tend to take those photos with a pinch of salt.

The platform adds two kinds of media to get past that. Live cameras show buildings as they are at this very moment. Videos tell the story of the district in a way that works well on a big touch screen.

## Live cameras

You can't stage a live feed. When a visitor opens a camera pointed at an entrance or a lobby, they see it exactly as it is right now: how well it's kept, how busy it is, and the people coming and going.

To open one, look for the camera tiles in the right panel of any building that already exists. Each tile is marked as live. Tap it and the camera opens full screen, showing its name, its location and its live status.

![a live camera open full screen, with its name and live status](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-cctv-enlarge.jpg)

## Videos

Videos pick up where the map leaves off. They show everyday life in the district, the events that happen there and the people who work there. They play without narration and hold attention well on a large screen, which suits someone standing at a stand with a few minutes to spare.

Tap a video tile in the right panel to start watching. To switch to another video, use the strip of thumbnails under the player.

![the video gallery open full screen, with the thumbnails below](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-video-enlarge.jpg)

## Why the media matters

Live cameras close the gap between what the marketing says and what's really there. The videos give people a sense of the district as a place where real companies work, not just a collection of buildings.

Together they also keep visitors at the screen for longer, and the longer someone stays, the more likely they are to keep exploring and send an enquiry.

Live feeds are always labelled as live, so nobody confuses a camera with a produced video. And whatever you open plays on top of the map and closes back to the same spot, so you never lose your place.
