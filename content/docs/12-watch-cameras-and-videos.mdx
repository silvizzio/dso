Camera feeds and videos provide another way to explore a property from the touch screen. Cameras are intended to show current activity in a particular location, while videos present recorded material about places, activities or events.

The prototype uses placeholder camera content and example videos. The labels demonstrate the intended interface, so a **Live** label on a sample camera does not mean that feed is connected.

## Opening a camera

Look for camera tiles in the right panel of an existing building. Tap a tile to open a larger viewer over the map, with the camera's name, location and live-status label.

![A camera viewer over the map, with its name and an illustrative live-status label](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-cctv-enlarge.jpg)

Once connected, a camera can show activity at the entrance, lobby or other area it covers. Its position limits what visitors can see; it gives a view of that location, rather than an account of everything happening in the building.

## Watching a video

Select a video tile to open the player over the map. Use the playback controls to watch it and the thumbnail strip below to switch between videos.

![The video player over the map, with playback controls and a thumbnail strip below](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-video-enlarge.jpg)

The footage shown here is an example for the prototype, not a verified tour of the selected DSO property. It needs to be replaced with the appropriate material before it can introduce that property to visitors.

## Returning to the property

Close either viewer to return to the same place on the map. You can then continue exploring the building or use Enquire where that action is available.

Camera and video labels should make clear whether visitors are seeing a connected feed, a recording or placeholder material. That distinction matters when using the media to answer questions about a property.
