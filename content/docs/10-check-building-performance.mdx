Most buildings are marketed on how they look.
How they run matters as much to a tenant, because it decides comfort, reliability and running costs.
That information usually stays inside the facilities team.

The platform opens it to visitors.
Building Info shows live data from the **building management system (BMS)**, the software that controls a building's cooling, lighting and power.
It turns that data into a small set of signals a visitor can read in seconds.

![live building data: overall health at the top, and each floor compared below](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-available-technohub-4-building-level.jpg)

## Live data shows what a brochure cannot

A brochure can describe a building as comfortable and efficient.
Live data shows whether it is, today.
A visitor sees whether the systems are monitored, whether its rooms are comfortable, and how much energy the building uses.

Live data is also hard to polish.
A monthly report can be edited before it is shared.
A live reading cannot, and visitors trust it for that reason.

## Reading the data takes four steps

1. Open Building Info from an available property.
2. Read the health signals at the top.
   Together they show whether the building's systems are monitored, whether its spaces are comfortable, and how much power it draws now.
3. Compare the floors in the charts below.
   Each chart shows one measure for every floor, from the ground floor up.
4. Look for the floor that stands out.
   - A warmer floor points to a question about cooling.
   - A floor that uses more energy points to a question about what drives the load.
   - An emptier floor may be where space is available.

## Floors are compared with each other, not with targets

The charts compare floors side by side rather than against a benchmark.
A visitor does not need to know what a good reading is.
An outlier is visible to anyone, and it leads straight to a useful question for the commercial team.

## Transparency differentiates the operator

For a tenant, operating quality becomes visible before signing.
A comfortable, monitored building is a better place to work, and usually a cheaper one to run.

For DSO, showing live performance signals confidence in how its buildings are run.
Few landlords open their building data to prospective tenants, which makes it a point of difference in a competitive market.

## Three design decisions shape the building view

- Signals, not a dashboard.
  A visitor is not a facilities manager, so the platform shows a few plain signals instead of every reading.
- Comparisons, not benchmarks.
  Comparing floors needs no expertise.
- Live, not reported.
  Current data persuades in a way a summary cannot.
