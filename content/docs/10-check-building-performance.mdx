Most buildings are marketed on how they look. But for a tenant, how a building runs matters just as much, because it affects comfort, reliability and running costs. Normally, that kind of information stays with the facilities team.

The platform shares it with visitors. Building Info shows live data from the **building management system (BMS)**, the software that controls a building's cooling, lighting and power. It condenses that data into a handful of simple signals that anyone can read in a few seconds.

![live building data: overall health at the top, and each floor compared below](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-available-technohub-4-building-level.jpg)

## Live data shows what a brochure cannot

A brochure can describe a building as comfortable and efficient. Live data shows whether it actually is, today. Visitors can see whether the building's systems are being monitored, whether its spaces are comfortable, and how much energy it uses.

Live data is also hard to dress up. A monthly report can be edited before anyone sees it, but a live reading cannot, and that is exactly why visitors trust it.

## How to read the data

1. Open Building Info from an available property.
2. Look at the health signals at the top. Together, they show whether the building's systems are monitored, whether its spaces are comfortable, and how much power it is drawing right now.
3. Compare the floors in the charts below. Each chart shows a single measure for every floor, from the ground floor up.
4. Look for any floor that stands out.
   - A warmer floor may raise a question about cooling.
   - A floor that uses more energy may raise a question about what is driving the load.
   - A floor with fewer people may be where space is available.

## Floors are compared with each other, not with targets

The charts compare floors side by side rather than against an industry benchmark. Visitors do not need to know what a "good" reading looks like. Anyone can spot a floor that stands out, and it leads naturally to a useful question for the commercial team.

## Transparency sets DSO apart

For tenants, it means they can see the quality of a building's operation before signing anything. A comfortable, well-monitored building is a better place to work, and usually a cheaper one to run as well.

For DSO, sharing live performance data shows confidence in how its buildings are managed. Very few landlords open up their building data to prospective tenants, which makes this a genuine point of difference in a competitive market.

## Design decisions behind the building view

- **Simple signals rather than a full dashboard.** Visitors are not facilities managers, so the platform shows a few clear signals instead of every available reading.
- **Comparisons rather than benchmarks.** Comparing floors with each other needs no specialist knowledge.
- **Live data rather than reports.** Current readings are far more persuasive than a summary.
