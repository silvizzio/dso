A building's appearance is only part of a tenant's decision.
How it operates affects comfort, reliability and running costs, but prospective tenants rarely see the information used by the facilities team.

Building Info is designed to present selected readings from the building management system (BMS), which controls cooling, lighting and power.
It summarises those readings for visitors who do not need a full facilities dashboard.
The figures currently shown are indicative until DSO connects the BMS.

![TechnoHub 4: floor uses and areas, with an occupancy comparison below](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-available-technohub-4-building-level.jpg)

## What operating data can tell a visitor

Once connected, current readings can add useful detail to a description of a building.
Visitors can look at system monitoring, comfort conditions and energy use, then ask the team about anything that needs explanation.

A reading needs context.
Differences between floors may reflect how the space is used, so the view is a starting point for questions rather than a complete assessment.

## Reading the view

Open Building Info from a property that offers the action.
The LIU 8 example shows that this information can also be available for a leased building.

![LIU 8 Building Info: system readings, use mix and floor details](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-liu-8-2.jpg)

The indicators at the top summarise the building's condition, including system monitoring, comfort and current power use.

Below the indicators, the use mix summarises how floor area is allocated.
The floor list runs from the highest level down to G and gives the use and area of each floor.
The TechnoHub 4 example also includes an occupancy chart for comparing floors.

Use the readings to prepare questions about cooling, power use or occupancy.
A low occupancy figure does not establish that space is available to lease; check the unit list and leasing status separately.

## Comparing floors

The occupancy chart compares floors within the building.
The floor list adds context by showing how those levels are used.
A difference alone does not explain its cause, which is why the view works best alongside a conversation with the team.

Only selected indicators appear here.
This keeps the information readable for prospective tenants while leaving detailed investigation to the people responsible for the building.

## Why DSO shares this information

For tenants, the view provides another way to assess a building before discussing a lease.
It helps them ask about the conditions and operating costs that will matter after they move in.

For DSO, sharing relevant data makes building management part of the property discussion.
The team can explain what the readings mean and how the building is operated, using the same view the visitor has seen.
