Some visitors need space in the next few months and cannot wait for District IO.
They need to identify suitable existing properties before arranging calls or site visits.

Now brings property status, unit sizes and leasing details together on the map.
Visitors can use that information to prepare a shortlist, then explore building data or an interior tour before enquiring.
The current availability and terms are indicative until DSO connects its leasing data.

![An available property, with unit sizes and status labels in the left panel](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-available-technohub-4.jpg)

## Reading availability on the map

Each property in Now has a status label.
Visitors can distinguish buildings with space offered for lease from those marked as leased or under offer before opening the details.

Selecting an available property opens its list of spaces in the left panel, with a size and status for each one.
The right panel shows leasing terms and building information for comparison.

## Preparing a shortlist

Start with a property marked Available in the Now overview.
Open it, compare the listed spaces by size and status, and read the terms in the right panel.

Use Building Info to examine the operating data view or View interior to look around the space.
These checks add context to a floor area: how the building operates and what the interior looks like.
Once you have a suitable option in mind, select Enquire to contact the team with the property details attached.

Keeping these tools close to the list lets visitors investigate a space without leaving the selection and starting again.

## Why spaces under offer remain visible

Under offer means an offer is in progress, but a lease has not yet been signed.
The space remains visible so visitors can understand its status and register interest if it becomes available again.

For the commercial team, these enquiries identify people to contact if the current offer does not proceed.
The label also distinguishes an offer in progress from a completed lease.

## Preparing for a viewing

A visitor can arrive at the first conversation with particular spaces and questions in mind.
The commercial team can then discuss those options and arrange a relevant viewing, with less time spent establishing the basic requirements.
