Not everyone can wait for District IO. Some visitors need space in the next few months, and for them the best option is whatever's free right now. Finding that usually takes a round of phone calls, emails and site visits before there's even a shortlist to talk about.

The platform cuts most of that out. Every property shows its status on the map, and opening an available one brings up a list of every space that can be leased. A visitor can put together a shortlist and check it properly, all in one visit.

![an available property opened: every space that can be leased, with its size and status](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-available-technohub-4.jpg)

## A live availability board

In the Now overview, every property has a status label. Scanning the map, a visitor can see at a glance which buildings have space and which are full, so the search is already narrowed down before they've opened anything.

Opening an available property turns the left panel into a list of its spaces, each with its size and status. Over on the right, the panel shows the leasing terms and the building details tenants usually compare first.

## From "is anything free?" to a shortlist

Start by scanning the status labels in the Now overview. Available properties are easy to spot. Open one and the left panel lists all its spaces. Compare the units by size and status, and read the terms in the right panel.

Then check your shortlist without leaving the screen. You can open the building's live data to see how it's running, or open the interior tour to look around the unit itself. When you've found the one, enquire about that specific space. The property details are attached automatically.

Those checks are only a tap away from the list, and that's deliberate. A shortlist someone has already tested against live data and a walk-through is a far better starting point for an enquiry than a list of floor areas.

## Why "under offer" spaces stay on the list

A space marked under offer has an offer in progress, but nobody has signed a lease yet. The platform shows these spaces instead of hiding them.

Partly that's because it shows there's real demand, which reflects well on the building. It also keeps the space open to enquiries. If the offer falls through, the commercial team already has the next interested person lined up.

## A shorter route to a viewing

For a tenant, going from "is anything free?" to a checked shortlist takes a few minutes, and they don't have to call anyone first.

For the commercial team, a visitor who arrives with a shortlist already has specific spaces in mind. That takes a lot of the back-and-forth out of the time between the first conversation and a site viewing.
