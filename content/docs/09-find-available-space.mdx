Some visitors need space soon and cannot wait for District IO to be built. For them, the quickest option is whatever is available right now. Normally, finding that space means a round of phone calls, emails and site visits before there is even a shortlist.

The platform makes this much quicker. Every property shows its status on the map, and opening an available property brings up a list of every space that can be leased. In a single visit, someone can put together a shortlist and check it properly.

![an available property opened: every space that can be leased, with its size and status](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-available-technohub-4.jpg)

## A live availability board

In the Now overview, every property has a status label. When visitors scan the map, they can see straight away which properties have space and which are full, so the search is narrowed down before they open anything.

Opening an available property turns the left panel into a list of its spaces, each with its size and status. The right panel shows the leasing terms and the building details that tenants usually compare first.

## Building and checking a shortlist in five steps

1. Look over the status labels in the Now overview. Available properties stand out clearly.
2. Open an available property. The left panel lists all its spaces.
3. Compare the units by size and status, and read the terms in the right panel.
4. Check your shortlist from the same screen.
   - Open the building's live data to see how it performs.
   - Open the interior tour to see the unit itself.
5. Enquire about a specific space, with the property details already attached.

These checks are deliberately placed just one tap away from the list. A shortlist that has been tested against live data and a virtual tour is a much stronger starting point for an enquiry than a list of floor areas.

## "Under offer" keeps near-misses in play

A space marked **under offer** has an offer in progress, but the lease has not been signed. The platform shows these spaces rather than hiding them, for two reasons.

- It tells visitors there is real demand, which strengthens the case for the building.
- It keeps the unit open for enquiries. If the offer falls through, the commercial team already has the next interested party lined up.

## A shorter leasing cycle

For tenants, getting from "is anything available?" to a tested shortlist takes minutes, and they do not need to contact anyone first.

For the commercial team, a visitor who has built a shortlist on the platform arrives at the first conversation with specific spaces in mind. That shortens the time between first contact and a site viewing.
