A fully leased building has nothing left to sell, so you might expect a sales platform to skip over it. The Digital Twin does the opposite and treats it as some of the best evidence it has.

A full building shows that DSO builds places companies want to move into and stay in. It gives a District IO buyer a real example of the standard they can expect. And a visitor who likes a full building is still a potential tenant, just for the next space that frees up.

![a leased building opened: its history, the units inside it and its live cameras](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-leased.jpg)

## What a full building tells you

In property, occupancy says a lot. If tenants picked a building and then renewed their leases, both the location and the way the building is run must be working for them. Someone weighing up an off-plan decision can read that straight off the map.

The platform adds more to it. Visitors can see how long the building has been running and what's inside it, so it's more than just a "full" label.

## Looking around a leased building

Select a leased building on the map. Its status label will tell you nothing is free at the moment.

The left panel shows the building's history, from when it was built up to today, along with the units inside and what each one is used for. In the right panel you can watch the building live through its cameras. If you like what you see, you can still enquire about future availability.

## Interest in a full building still counts

For a visitor, a full building settles the question of whether people actually want to be here. It also shows what a building looks like after DSO has been running it for years, which is useful for anyone thinking about District IO.

For the commercial team, an enquiry about a full building is still worth having. They can offer the next space that becomes free there, or suggest something similar elsewhere in the district. That's why the Enquire button stays on every leased building, even when there's no unit to offer right now.
