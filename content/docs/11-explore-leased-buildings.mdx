A fully leased building has no space to sell.
On a sales platform it could easily be treated as a dead end.
The Digital Twin treats it as evidence instead.

A full building shows that DSO builds places tenants choose and stay in.
It also shows the standard a District IO tenant can expect.
And a visitor who likes a full building is still a potential tenant, for the next space that becomes free.

![a leased building opened: its history, the units inside it and its live cameras](/dso/api/img/04-now-lod3-leased.jpg)

## Full buildings prove demand and quality

Occupancy is one of the strongest signals in property.
If tenants chose a building and renewed their leases, the location and the operation work.
A visitor weighing an off-plan decision reads that signal directly from the map.

The platform strengthens it with history and live views.
A visitor sees how long the building has run and what it holds, not only that it is full.

## Exploring a leased building takes five steps

1. Select a leased building.
   Its status label shows that no space is free now.
2. Read its history in the left panel, from construction to today.
3. See what is inside: the units in the building, each with its use and status.
4. Watch it live through the building's cameras in the right panel.
5. Enquire for future availability.

## Interest in a full building is still a lead

For a visitor, a full building answers the question of demand.
It also shows the quality of a building DSO has run for years, which matters to anyone considering District IO.

For the commercial team, an enquiry about a full building is useful.
The team can offer the next space that frees up there, or a similar space elsewhere in the district.

## Two design decisions shape this view

- History, not only status.
  How long a building has run, and what it contains, says more about quality than a single label.
- No dead ends.
  Enquire stays available on every full building, because the visitor's interest is real even when no unit is free.
