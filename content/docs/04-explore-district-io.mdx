District IO combines offices, techno labs, homes, retail, a hotel and public space on one site.
Different visitors come for different parts of it.
A lab tenant, a home buyer and a hotel operator each care about a different corner of the complex.

Shown as one block, the complex is too large to judge.
So the platform uses the **phase** as the unit of exploration.
A phase is a part of District IO that is designed, built and handed over as one package.
A visitor finds the phase that matches their needs in three taps and can ignore the rest.

## The phase is the right unit of exploration

The platform could have divided District IO by building, by use or by floor.
The phase wins for three reasons.

1. It matches how the complex is delivered.
   Each phase has its own completion date, so choosing a phase also means choosing a moment of arrival.
2. Each phase has a distinct purpose.
   Some phases are mainly workplace, one holds the homes, and one holds the destinations.
   The purpose tells a visitor at once whether a phase is relevant.
3. It matches how the complex is sold.
   Leasing and sales conversations happen phase by phase, so a visitor who explores by phase already speaks the language of the commercial team.

## Exploring by phase takes three taps

1. Select District IO, on the map or in the list of areas of interest.
   - The map flies to the complex.
   - A marker appears for each phase.
   - The left panel becomes the list of phases, in delivery order.

![District IO selected: a marker for each phase on the map, and the list of phases in the left panel](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io.jpg)

2. Read the list as a sequence.
   Each phase shows a short summary and its expected completion.
   The order itself explains the complex: it starts as a place to work and live, and grows into a destination.
3. Select a phase, in the list or on the map.
   - The map moves to the phase and shows what is inside it.
   - The right panel explains the phase.

![a phase selected: its parts on the map, and the right panel explaining what the phase is for](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-1a.jpg)

From a selected phase, a visitor has three onward paths.
They can step through the delivery years to see when it arrives, go down to its buildings, or enquire about it.

## The right panel explains a phase in order of relevance

The panel for a phase answers three questions, in the order a visitor asks them.

1. What is this phase for?
   The panel lists what the phase contains, so a visitor can tell a workplace phase from a residential one immediately.
2. How large is it?
   The panel shows the total floor area and the leasable area.
   **Gross floor area (GFA)** counts all the floor inside the buildings.
   **Gross leasable area (GLA)** counts only the part a tenant can rent, which is the figure that matters for a lease.
3. Where does it stand?
   The panel shows the expected delivery and the current design stage, so a visitor never mistakes a plan for a finished building.

Purpose comes first because it decides relevance.
Size and status only matter once a visitor knows the phase is for them.

## Exploring by phase benefits both sides of the sale

For a buyer, a large complex becomes something they can navigate.
They skip the phases that do not concern them and spend their time on the one that does.

For the commercial team, the phase a visitor opens is a signal of intent.
It shows what the visitor is interested in before they say a word.
When the visitor enquires, that choice travels with the enquiry.

## Four design decisions shape this flow

- The map and the list stay in sync, so visitors can navigate by place or by name.
- The list follows delivery order, so reading it top to bottom also explains how the complex grows.
- Each phase leads with its purpose, then its figures.
- Every phase shows its status, so the projected nature of the figures is never hidden.
