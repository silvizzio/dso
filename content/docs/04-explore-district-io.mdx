District IO brings together offices, techno labs, homes, retail, a hotel and public space on a single site. Different visitors are drawn to different parts of it. A lab tenant, a home buyer and a hotel operator will each be interested in a completely different corner of the complex.

Presented as one large block, the complex is simply too big to take in. So the platform uses the **phase** as the way to explore it. A phase is a section of District IO that is designed, built and handed over as a single package. In just three taps, a visitor can find the phase that matches what they need and set the rest aside.

## Why the phase is the right way to explore

The platform could have split District IO by building, by type of use or by floor. The phase works best for three reasons.

1. **It reflects how the complex will be delivered.** Each phase has its own completion date, so choosing a phase also means choosing when you would arrive.
2. **Each phase has a clear purpose.** Some are mainly workplaces, one contains the homes, and another holds the leisure and visitor destinations. That purpose tells a visitor right away whether a phase is relevant to them.
3. **It reflects how the complex will be sold.** Leasing and sales discussions happen phase by phase, so a visitor who explores this way is already speaking the same language as the commercial team.

## Exploring by phase takes three taps

1. Select District IO, either on the map or in the list of areas of interest.
   - The map flies to the complex.
   - A marker appears for each phase.
   - The left panel turns into a list of phases, in the order they will be delivered.

![District IO selected: a marker for each phase on the map, and the list of phases in the left panel](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io.jpg)

2. Read the list from top to bottom. Each phase shows a short summary and its expected completion. The order itself tells the story of the complex: it begins as a place to work and live, and grows into a destination.
3. Select a phase, either in the list or on the map.
   - The map moves to that phase and shows what it contains.
   - The right panel describes the phase in more detail.

![a phase selected: its parts on the map, and the right panel explaining what the phase is for](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-1a.jpg)

Once a phase is selected, the visitor has three ways forward. They can step through the delivery years to see when it will be ready, drill down into its buildings, or make an enquiry about it.

## What the right panel tells you about a phase

The panel answers three questions, in the order a visitor naturally asks them.

1. **What is this phase for?** The panel lists what the phase contains, so a visitor can immediately tell a workplace phase from a residential one.
2. **How big is it?** The panel shows both the total floor area and the leasable area. **Gross floor area (GFA)** covers all the floor space inside the buildings. **Gross leasable area (GLA)** covers only the space a tenant can actually rent, which is the figure that matters when it comes to a lease.
3. **Where does it stand?** The panel shows the expected delivery date and the current design stage, so no one mistakes a plan for a finished building.

Purpose comes first because it tells the visitor whether the phase is relevant at all. Size and status only matter once they know the phase is right for them.

## How this benefits both sides of the sale

For a buyer, it turns a very large complex into something easy to navigate. They can skip the phases that do not concern them and spend their time on the one that does.

For the commercial team, the phase a visitor opens is a clear signal of intent. It shows what they are interested in before they have said a word, and when they make an enquiry, that information travels with it.

## Design decisions behind this flow

- The map and the list stay in sync, so visitors can navigate either by location or by name.
- The list follows the delivery order, so reading from top to bottom also shows how the complex will grow.
- Each phase leads with its purpose, followed by its figures.
- Every phase displays its status, so it is always clear that the figures are projections.
