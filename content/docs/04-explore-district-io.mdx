District IO combines offices, techno labs, homes, shops, a hotel and public space.
Visitors will be interested in different parts of that mix.
A company looking for lab space needs a different starting point from a family looking for a home or a hotel operator.

The platform organises the complex by phase so visitors can focus on the areas relevant to them.
Each phase groups work that will be designed, built and handed over together.

## Why the platform starts with phases

Each phase has a target year and mix of uses.
Choosing a phase helps visitors narrow down both the type of space they want and when it may be ready.

Some phases focus on workplaces, while others contain homes or visitor destinations.
These differences make the phase list a useful starting point before examining individual buildings and floors.
The sales and leasing team also discusses District IO by phase, so enquiries use the same structure.

## Choosing a phase

Select District IO on the map or in the list of areas of interest.
The map moves to the complex and places a marker on each phase.
The left panel lists the phases with a short summary and target year for each one.
Map markers pair the phase name with Planned and its year, such as Planned 2030.
The label describes the proposal; it does not mean construction is complete.

![District IO selected, with phase markers on the map and the phase list on the left](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io.jpg)

Read the list to compare each phase's uses and target year.
The current example groups offices and labs in Phase 1A, commercial space and a retail promenade in 1B, a hotel and convention centre in 1C, residential and social space in 1D, and a museum, central plaza and residential towers in Phase 2.
Several phases share a delivery year, so compare their uses as well as their dates.
Select a phase from the list or map to examine it more closely.
The map shows its parts, and the right panel describes the phase.

![A selected phase, with its parts on the map and its details in the right panel](/dso/api/img/03-future-lod3-district-io-1a.jpg)

From here, you can explore the buildings, check the delivery years or enquire about the phase.

## Reading the phase details

The right panel starts with the phase's uses, helping visitors decide whether it is relevant before comparing its size.
It then shows two area measures.

Gross floor area (GFA) describes the total floor space inside the buildings.
Gross leasable area (GLA) describes the space available to lease.
For a prospective tenant, GLA is the relevant measure when discussing how much space they could rent.

The panel also shows Target Year and Status.
Target Year is the proposed timing, while a status such as Concept describes the design stage.
These details explain how far the proposal has progressed and help distinguish planned space from completed buildings.

## How this helps the visitor and the team

Visitors can spend time on the phases that match their needs without opening every building in the complex.
If they enquire, the selected phase is attached to the form, giving the commercial team useful context for a reply.

The map and list stay linked so visitors can navigate by location or name.
Delivery order makes the development sequence easier to follow, while status labels explain the stage each phase has reached.
Projected and indicative figures remain identified as such.
